**Status:** Executed geographic and numerical sensitivity experiment. These are sites for further investigation, not validated lunar or Martian analogs. The original 64-candidate ranking, earlier regional catalogs, production ranking, and frontend were not modified.

## Geographic screening and uncertainty

For every original and shifted regional leader, the output records complete 1:10m mapped-land coverage, no mapped lake/glaciated-area intersection, all 12,100 bilinear source-node checks passing, approximate footprint-to-boundary distance, and original/shifted footprint IoU. Distance uses a spherical equal-area plane to **screen proximity**; it is not a measured coastline or glacier-margin positional accuracy. Mask geometries are clipped to each 10-degree source tile: artificial tile edges are excluded from the reported mapped-land boundary distance and reported separately. The nearest feature outside a tile is unknown, so distances to lakes and glaciers *within* a tile are not global minimum distances. The two Natural Earth map scales share lineage and are not independent ground truth. Seasonal water, current snow and ice, small unmapped lakes, and ETOPO1 source-resolution variation remain unresolved.

The original East African Tycho/Gale regional leader at **0.9986°N, 35°E** has an approximate mapped-lake separation of **0.8 km from the 220 km footprint boundary**; the shifted Tranquillitatis leader at **1.4984°N, 35.5°E** is about **4.0 km** from a mapped lake. Small boundary differences or unmapped seasonal water could change their eligibility. Within the clipped tile, the South American combined Tranquillitatis first-place footprint is approximately **82 km** from a mapped lake and **333 km** from a mapped land/water boundary; the shifted first place is approximately **239 km** and **278 km** away respectively. The old apparent land-boundary values of 215/256 km were distances to the **artificial tile edge** and must not be interpreted as coastline proximity. The original/shifted Jezero combined pair is approximately **91/154 km** from mapped lakes; the shifted window is about **49 km from the tile edge**, not 49 km from a mapped coastline. These distances do not prove a dry or ice-free surface. The two South American combined Tycho/Gale leaders do not overlap and their shifted center is approximately **54 km** from mapped lakes and **128 km** from mapped glaciated-area polygons. A nearest mapped feature is a warning for targeted verification, not an accuracy or confidence estimate.

The original expansion's 1:50m versus 1:10m comparison changed **13 South American** and **6 East African** eligibility classes. This screen uses 1:10m but still has no independent lake/glacier ground truth. See the earlier [robustness report](discovery-robustness-and-expansion.md) for the precise class changes.

## Candidate selection for investigation

No first-ranked center qualifies as a **stable, validated analog**. Select small, explicitly conditional *study units*:

1. **Tranquillitatis terrain pair (priority A):** the South American regional leaders `south-america-elevation-gradient-r02-c04` (21.9726°S, 67°W) and `south-america-elevation-gradient-shift-r01-c04` (22.4766°S, 66.5°W). Their 220 km windows share about 0.400 IoU, have no mapped land/water/ice conflict under the tested screen, and the reference is within each separate regional descriptor range. The original combined first place is elsewhere, so investigate these **as a pair** to test whether the shared terrain and its geological materials support any useful interpretation. Do not present either center as the single best analog.
2. **Jezero instability pair (priority B):** `south-america-elevation-gradient-r05-c06` (18.9840°S, 65°W) and `south-america-elevation-gradient-shift-r03-c06` (20.4710°S, 64.5°W). These are distinct windows (IoU about 0.105), both selected under the two combined configurations and within the separate descriptor ranges. Their poor overlap makes them a useful **methodological comparison**, not a stable location. Confirm land/water classification and source terrain detail before spending effort on composition.
3. **East African mask-boundary control (priority C):** `east-africa-inland-water-gradient-r03-c03` (0.9986°N, 35°E). Its footprint is mapped as eligible but only about 0.8 km from a mapped lake. Select it solely for an independent water-boundary check; it is **not** shortlisted as a planetary analog.

Tycho and Gale have no endorsed terrestrial candidate for site characterization in this phase: their top positions jump to nonoverlapping footprints and still extrapolate in slope and RMS. Tranquillitatis and Jezero remain exploratory questions, with the paired windows explicitly representing uncertainty. Geographic diversity is provided by the East African **validation control**, not by equating its topographic score to the South American studies.

## Reproduction and limits

The comparison files `results/terrestrial-discovery-characterization-v1/south-america-elevation-gradient-shifted.json`, `east-africa-inland-water-gradient-shifted.json`, `combined-shifted.json`, and `summary.json` include source IDs, complete generated/excluded/eligible catalogs, reference features, regional Earth IQRs, all four full rankings, feature contributions, range diagnostics, overlap and mapped-feature distances. They are versioned separately from all prior results. Run from the development-copy root:

```powershell
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe backend/discovery_characterization.py
backend/.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m unittest discover -s backend -p test_discovery_characterization.py -v
Get-FileHash results/terrestrial-discovery-expansion-v1/combined.json -Algorithm SHA256
```

This is a **selection for further characterization only**. The candidate catalog is small, geographically selected and overlapping. A center shift also changes which candidates qualify and their Earth IQR. Land/water masks are generalized, dated or incomplete; ETOPO1 grid spacing is not effective terrain resolution; the planetary and terrestrial elevation products differ in source and reference surface. No probability, accuracy, analog score or mission-safety conclusion follows from these rankings.
