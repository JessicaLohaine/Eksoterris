# Global terrestrial topographic search: design for review

**Status:** proposed design; no global search, new ranking, or new analog discovery has been implemented. This document concerns candidate discovery only. Production still uses seven selected terrestrial windows.

## 1. Executive summary

Use a versioned, locally readable **NOAA ETOPO1 Ice Surface, authoritative grid-registered** global elevation raster for an initial, explicitly exploratory search. Generate deterministic candidate centers on an equal-area global grid, classify the full 220 km footprint with independent land/water and ice evidence, extract the existing three descriptors, and precompute an immutable feature catalog. Rank its eligible windows against the four existing planetary references with the existing Earth-only IQR-scaled distance, while keeping the seven-window result separately reproducible. Validate raster registration, mask geometry, spatial coverage, scale sensitivity, and ranking stability before exposing any global result. Targeted higher-detail checks may follow; a nearest global window still does **not** establish a validated lunar or Martian analog.

This proposal requires decisions about the land policy, center density, output retention, and validation criteria before implementation. The numeric center counts below are estimates from grid geometry, **not** processing benchmarks or observed discoveries.

## 2. Verified repository baseline and limits

[`backend/science.py`](../backend/science.py) defines the fixed four planetary reference regions and seven Earth candidate centers. Its default regional window uses 11 by 11 output cells at 20 km center spacing, with 25 bilinear raster queries averaged per cell. The nominal support is approximately 220 km across. It extracts P95 minus P5 elevation, median interior slope in degrees, and root-mean-square (RMS) residual after fitting a plane. [`backend/terrain_knn.py`](../backend/terrain_knn.py) fits each descriptor's median and interquartile range (IQR) on **Earth candidates only** and computes

`distance(E, P) = sqrt(mean_j(((E_j - P_j) / IQR_E,j)^2))`.

It ranks by increasing distance with deterministic identifier tie-breaking, reports per-descriptor range extrapolation, and exposes each descriptor's contribution to squared distance. Distance is unbounded; it is not a probability or percentage. A reference within all three individual Earth ranges may still be far from every Earth vector in three-dimensional feature space. Zero or effectively zero IQR and nonfinite descriptors currently prevent a comparison; a larger catalog needs the same explicit failure, not a silent replacement scale.

[`backend/sources.py`](../backend/sources.py) requests a separate NOAA ERDDAP `etopo180.csv` regional subset around each Earth center, parses the CSV into an in-memory raster, and caches the exact response bytes and hash. It also acquires NASA LOLA `LDEM_4` and MOLA `MEGT90N000CB`. Per-center CSV requests cannot be extrapolated safely into a reproducible thousands-of-windows global batch. The current `fetch` limit of 40 MB also precludes using that helper unchanged for a global raster. NASA POWER is context only; SRTMGL1 is not in the ranking. The recent [geospatial](geospatial-validation.md), [window-level](independent-window-validation.md), [scale](spatial-scale-experiment.md), and [LOLA count](lola-observation-count-validation.md) reports establish numerical reproducibility and important observational limits. They do not validate analog status. Tycho and Gale extrapolate on the seven-window catalog; Tranquillitatis is sensitive to sampling choices.

## 3. Global data strategy

NOAA describes [ETOPO1](https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/mgg/global/relief/ETOPO1/tiled/) as a global, one-arc-minute composite of land topography and ocean bathymetry. Its Ice Surface edition represents the **top** of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets. The authoritative grid/node-registered version has 21,601 by 10,801 nodes, WGS 84 geographic coordinates, and heights relative to sea level; the cell-registered version is derived and slightly flattens relief. Download one published, identified grid-registered Ice Surface product in a format with checked node registration; record the exact URL, release, SHA-256 and metadata. The present ERDDAP `etopo180` service is attributed in the repository to ETOPO1, but equivalence between its regional values/registration and the chosen global file must be **independently checked** at all seven existing windows before a new catalog is trusted. Do not assume that equal nominal arc-minute spacing proves equal bytes or registration.

The [NOAA technical report](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-04/ETOPO1.pdf) describes a heterogeneous source mosaic. One arc-minute is grid spacing, not globally uniform effective terrain resolution or measurement accuracy. A single elevation raster does not reliably distinguish land from ocean: below-sea-level dry land exists. A separate, versioned coastline and inland-water polygon layer is needed for footprint classification. [GSHHG version 2.3.7](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/pwessel/gshhg/) supplies hierarchical WGS 84 coastlines, lakes, and islands; its Antarctic ice-front and grounding-line boundaries are **not** a global glacier/ice-sheet classification. Compare the chosen intermediate-resolution polygons with a finer level at difficult shorelines; record the version, LGPL terms, and antimeridian-split shapefiles. A mask is an eligibility/quality input, **not** a fourth similarity descriptor. Use independent ice evidence before calling an ETOPO1 Ice Surface footprint exposed bedrock.

### Independent ice classification

For a **bounded Greenland control**, evaluate NASA/NSIDC [GIMP land-ice and ocean masks, NSIDC-0714 version 1](https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0714/versions/1), initially the documented **2015, 90 m GeoTIFF ice mask**. Its coverage is approximately 59–84°N and 89°W–8°E, in WGS 84 / NSIDC Polar Stereographic North (EPSG:3413). The collection also offers 2000 and, per the updated collection page, 2020 masks; verify each actual file's metadata before using it. The older [user guide](https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/nsidc-0714-v001-userguide_0.pdf) documents 2000/2015 and warns of a bad projection header in the **2000 grounded-ice file**, which should not be substituted for the 2015 ice mask. An ice mask flags mapped glacier ice, not a measured elevation error, seasonal snow history, or a global ice-free guarantee. Its companion ocean mask should not be mistaken for a land/water/ice multiclass map.

For an **Antarctic control**, evaluate NASA/NSIDC [MEaSUREs Antarctic Boundaries, NSIDC-0709 version 2](https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0709/versions/2). The [version-2 guide](https://nsidc.org/sites/default/files/nsidc-0709-v002-userguide.pdf) describes 500 m mask pixels (0 ocean, 125 floating ice, 255 grounded ice) and vector ice-front, grounding-line and shelf boundaries in EPSG:3031, approximately 60–90°S. These data derive mostly from 2008–2009 boundaries, with some other observations; rapidly evolving margins and missing pinning points are documented. The raster classes do not separately certify exposed rock/nunataks; a pixel outside the mapped ice classes must not automatically be admitted as exposed land. NSIDC access requires a free Earthdata account. A later targeted check could use the [ITS_LIVE annual Antarctic ice-extent masks, NSIDC-0794 version 1](https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0794/versions/1), but that 240 m, 24-time-step netCDF is too large for this bounded pilot without a verified subsetting service.

Outside those two ice sheets, [Randolph Glacier Inventory (RGI), NSIDC-0770 version 7](https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0770/versions/7) supplies regional WGS 84 glacier outlines of variable mapping detail, intended as a roughly year-2000 snapshot and explicitly excluding the Greenland/Antarctic ice sheets. One relevant regional package may be evaluated for a glacier-edge control if its size fits the pilot budget. It does not classify seasonal snow, current glacier margins or uncharted small ice. [Natural Earth 1:10m Glaciated Areas version 4.0.0](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-glaciated-areas/) is a small screening alternative, **not** a definitive ice mask: it explicitly includes recently deglaciated areas and some old outlines. With no verified local ice coverage, classify the footprint as *ice status unresolved* and keep it out of the exposed-land pilot ranking. Seasonal snow remains unresolved by these static masks; absence from a dated outline is not proof of year-round ice-free ground. The proposed catalog is not a seasonal surface-state classifier.

For spatial overlay, preserve ETOPO1's WGS 84 node grid and each mask's native CRS. Transform query footprints or sample coordinates into EPSG:3413/3031 for regional ice tests, with independent round-trip controls; calculate area on the ellipsoid or in a validated local equal-area projection. Treat categorical masks with nearest-class/area-intersection logic, **never** bilinear interpolation; compare original boundaries with ETOPO1 grid-node neighborhoods at margins. Record temporal mismatch and NoData outside each mask's footprint. Do not label an absence of evidence as exposed land.

[ETOPO 2022](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ngdc.mgg.dem:etopo_2022/html) offers newer source material, 15-arc-second tiles, coarser distributed variants, and source-ID products. Its potential provenance advantage warrants a *controlled, targeted comparison* (possibly a coarser 60-arc-second variant), not an immediate global high-resolution download or an unnoticed replacement of ETOPO1. Different versions must generate different catalog IDs and recalibrated IQRs. [NASA Earthdata describes SRTMGL1](https://search.earthdata.nasa.gov/search?q=SRTMGL1) as covering land between 60°N and 56°S, so it is unsuitable as the sole global discovery source; it could later validate a covered shortlisted site, after authenticated data access is proven. No alternative source is proposed to change the present Moon/Mars references.

Ocean elevations must never enter a land-only terrain descriptor through a coastal interpolation kernel. Detect source NoData, nonfinite values, and any special fill codes from the *selected file's actual metadata*. Global geographic completeness does not guarantee uniform observational support. Record excluded windows and reasons. Islands, coasts and polar/ice regions remain indexed and auditable, even if separated from the initial all-land ranking. Antimeridian reads must wrap the global raster consistently, and polar footprints require a geodesic construction rather than a `1/cos(latitude)` longitude margin.

## 4. Candidate generation and geographic coverage

A fixed angular latitude/longitude lattice oversamples high latitudes by area. Propose centers at every pixel center of a deterministic [HEALPix equal-area partition](https://healpix.sourceforge.io/doc/html/intro_Geometric_Algebraic_Propert.htm), with a fixed `Nside`, numbering scheme, geographic center and stable ID. HEALPix supplies equal-area **cells**, not equal physical nearest-center distances or a proof that every 220 km landscape is represented. Generate the grid once, save the center/footprint manifest, and reuse the same manifest across four references and controlled source comparisons. No famous region enters candidate generation by name. All eligible centers, including unremarkable ones, must be processed with the same rules.

For discussion, `Npix = 12 Nside²`: `Nside=16` yields 3,072 global centers; `32` yields 12,288; `64` yields 49,152. The area-equivalent square-root spacing on a 6,371 km *illustrative spherical Earth* is approximately 408, 204, and 102 km, respectively. It is **not** exact neighbor spacing. A 220 km window at 32 risks coarse center coverage; 64 provides more overlapping windows and greater workload. Evaluate both in a bounded pilot and map the maximum geodesic distance from eligible land locations to the nearest eligible center. Test shifted grids or a finer level if coverage is inadequate. A 16 grid is primarily a throughput/geometry pilot, not a defensible final search density. The center-density decision requires measured candidate coverage and ranking stability, not an arbitrary winner count.

Build each 220 km footprint with the existing Earth spherical-local construction for the first equivalence experiment and compare it with independent geodesic/appropriate local-projection geometry, especially at high latitude. Before global adoption, determine where the existing approximation fails relative to the chosen footprint and source-node spacing; document the tested tolerance and rationale. Handle `[-180, 180)` output longitudes and source-specific `[0, 360)` indexing explicitly. A window crossing the antimeridian remains **one** window. At the poles, longitude is singular: do not extrapolate the existing regional CSV margin formula. Reject out-of-bounds footprints with a recorded reason or use an independently validated polar geometry.

Compute land fraction as an area fraction of the **whole footprint** against a coastline/inland-water polygon, using geodesic area or a locally suitable equal-area projection. Define denominator and mask version. Exposed land excludes inland water and verified ice. Record separate geodesic fractions for dry land, open ocean, inland water, mapped land ice, floating ice and unclassified area; they must sum to the window area within a documented numerical area tolerance. An elevation sign test or land at 121 output points is insufficient. The original 25-query bilinear kernel can cross a shoreline even when its query point is land: examine every source node with nonzero interpolation weight, not only the query coordinate. A coastline-intersecting footprint with dry-land interpolated heights is still mixed. Do not fill invalid land/ocean samples or average across a class boundary for the exposed-land analysis.

Use mutually exclusive primary statuses with recorded secondary fractions/reasons: `invalid_footprint`, `insufficient_valid_elevation`, `ice_covered`, `mixed_land_water`, `ocean_dominated`, `exposed_land_candidate`, plus `ice_status_unresolved` when the required ice coverage is absent. A useful precedence is invalid geometry first; missing raster support next; ice/unresolved ice next; then water composition; exposed land last. The pilot's **strict experimental cohort** contains only complete dry-land footprints with no mapped ice in the specified inventory epochs with all required elevation queries and nonzero-weight source nodes on verified land. This is a conservative comparison policy, not an established scientific land-fraction threshold. Windows with verified ice overlap are described separately as ice-covered; coast/lake/island windows are kept for diagnostics, and all-ocean footprints never enter the exposed-land ranking. `ocean_dominated` is descriptive only when a declared experimental water-area rule has been selected; otherwise store the exact fractions and label any land/water mixture as mixed. Mask boundary uncertainty and floating-point intersection tolerance require explicit validation, not a hidden epsilon.

Record query-level mask class, 3,025 expected query count, 121 expected cell count, 81 expected interior slope cells, missing/NoData values, mask-unresolved fraction, and whether a kernel touched water. The existing production extractor currently expects a complete finite grid: a few finite cells alone do **not** meet its descriptor requirements. Compare strict complete-land with *declared experimental* land-fraction and valid-coverage parameters (for example 100% versus 95% land area, and all queries versus a proposed partial-valid rule), listing exactly which windows change category and whether percentiles/slope/RMS remain mathematically defined. These example values are stress-test parameters, **not** validated acceptance cutoffs; a partial-valid descriptor method would be a distinct methodology and cannot silently enter production. No-data values may never be fabricated by cross-coast interpolation.

### Measurable center coverage

For both `Nside=32` and `64`, report the exact theoretical center count, bounded-pilot center IDs, eligible counts per category and region, and an independently constructed set of evaluation points on eligible land within the pilot tiles. Compute each point's ellipsoidal/geodesic distance to its nearest **eligible** center, with missing centers reported as uncovered. Publish distribution (minimum, median, P95, maximum) and map of gaps by predefined latitude band, continent or island group; report numerator and denominator for every stratum. Report physical center density per eligible km², pairwise footprint intersection-over-union/overlap area, and near-duplicate counts for a declared overlap parameter. Compare the same evaluation points for 32, 64 and a reproducibly rotated grid; include island and high-latitude coverage. Equal-area HEALPix cells do not guarantee equal nearest-center distances or adequate land coverage.

The pilot may measure these diagnostics **within its declared regional bounds only**. Selecting a final global grid requires a later globe-wide coverage audit of eligible land, coastal islands and ice uncertainty; a pilot cannot claim global completeness. Do not adopt a universal maximum acceptable center gap. Show area left without a center for several *explicit experimental gap values*, selected relative to the 220 km footprint and grid geometry, and obtain a mission-specific decision before using one as a pass/fail condition.

Overlapping windows are expected, especially at 64. Preserve raw window ranks and distances. A later display can group substantially overlapping footprints into geographic clusters and show representative windows without changing the mathematical ranking. Define the grouping by measured footprint overlap, disclose the rule, and count distinct regions separately from overlapping windows. Do not interpret densely sampled regions as independent evidence or winner frequency as probability.

## 5. Spatial support, extraction and ranking

Keep the current 220 km, 11 by 11, 25-query-per-cell calculation as a **frozen baseline experiment**, not as a claim of equal source resolution. Existing approximate equatorial source spacings are 7.58 km for lunar LOLA, 14.82 km for Martian MOLA, and 1.85 km north-south for ETOPO1; actual effective detail varies with source processing and latitude. The 4 km subcell query spacing is numerical interpolation/averaging, not new observation density. A global Earth catalog should use the same intended physical footprint and descriptor definitions as the seven-site baseline. Prove global raster/csv consistency first; retain separate output versions if any registration or processing correction is justified.

Two controlled experiments precede a production choice: (1) hold geographic width approximately 220 km and change **output sampling density** using the already documented 11, 7 and 5 grids; (2) retain 20 km output spacing and compare **physical window extent** of 180, 220 and 260 km. Native source-node spacing, effective source resolution, and aggregation support are separate quantities. The seven-site experiment showed that these choices can move winners, notably Tranquillitatis, and five cells per side can weaken slope estimation. No interpolation or aggregation manufactures observations. Do not choose scale from a preferred Earth winner. Flag configurations without sufficient valid samples instead of filling them.

### Controlled coarse aggregation of the same Earth source

In a separate pilot artifact, compare the unchanged ETOPO1 1-arc-minute node grid with a **proposed 4-arc-minute block-support representation** derived exclusively from the same ETOPO1 Ice Surface bytes. Four arc-minutes is a stress-test parameter (about four times the source meridional node interval), **not** a recommended effective resolution or a matched planetary resolution. Predeclare the block boundaries and geographic centers; integrate/area-weight the source's reconstructed surface over each physical 4' by 4' block, preserving units and registration, and store the resulting block means on an explicitly cell-registered grid. Record block area, included node support, aggregation algorithm, treatment of edge/polar/antimeridian blocks, source and derived hashes. Never turn an incomplete or land/ocean-crossing block into an apparently valid exposed-land value. A simpler fixed-node 4-by-4 mean must not be called area-preserving without checking its node support and latitude weights.

For the seven unchanged Earth centers, sample the same 220 km footprints/output-cell centers and independently recompute P95-P5, slope and detrended RMS from the coarse representation. Hold the four original planetary references fixed. First compare Earth descriptors; then rank with the **fixed original seven-window IQR** to isolate representation effects, and separately with the newly fitted seven-window IQR to expose calibration effects. Report ranks, distances, feature contributions and extrapolation under each, clearly labeling their calibrations. A second coarsening level may be considered only if the first pilot explains a need. Map local lost/changed variation at the same physical positions, especially slope and RMS. The coarse experiment cannot replace the seven-window baseline or claim a preferred aggregation scale; it asks how much information this source representation discards.

Fit the existing Earth-only median/IQR scaling **afresh and once** for each frozen, eligible catalog version. Expanding seven windows to thousands changes each IQR and hence all distances; the new numbers must never be shown as directly comparable with the old metric calibration. Report the catalog size, center scheme, eligible footprint policy, source hashes, medians, IQRs and rejected features. Check near-zero IQR explicitly. Spatially clustered overlap and rare extremes can shift calibration, so compare IQR and ranks under center density, geographic block/continent holdouts, and policy choices; do not silently trim extremes or learn weights. The existing three dimensions are potentially correlated and remain a deliberately limited baseline, not three independent kinds of evidence.

For every reference, retain the complete rank order, original descriptors, scaled differences, numerical squared-distance contributions, and one-dimensional range diagnostics. Preserve the interpretation “nearest among evaluated windows.” Range containment alone cannot establish joint support; even a globally nearest point may still be a poor analog. Report instability, gaps in coverage, and quality exclusions; no distance cutoff, confidence percentage, or probabilistic analog label is justified without external reference cases and an independently evaluated scientific criterion.

## 6. Proposed computation and provenance architecture

Implement only after design approval, as an **offline, resumable batch** beside the current FastAPI service: acquisition/manifest → validated windowed raster accessor and land mask → deterministic center/eligibility generator → existing-descriptor extractor → immutable feature catalog → existing distance/range diagnostics against four references → read-only API artifact. Do not make a web request run a global scan, load the complete global raster for each center, or modify the existing seven-site endpoint until the catalog passes regression checks. Reuse production feature definitions through a separately verified adapter after the independent checks; keep independent calculations in validation code.

Estimated raw ETOPO1 node count is `21,601 × 10,801 = 233,312,401`. At two bytes per node, that is about **467 MB decimal / 445 MiB** uncompressed; a float64 copy would be about **1.87 GB decimal** before masks/working arrays. Actual download size, compressed I/O, memory and runtime are **unmeasured**. Read source tiles/windows lazily or memory-map a validated representation; group nearby windows by geographic tile and include interpolation halos, wrapping the seam. The current 40 MB fetch limit and per-window CSV materialization are unsuitable here. A 220 km, 11 by 11 window nominally needs `121 × 25 = 3,025` bilinear queries. Before land exclusions and cache reuse, 12,288 centers imply about **37.2 million** queries and 49,152 imply **148.7 million**; each bilinear query accesses up to four nodes. These are upper-stage work estimates, not network calls or timed benchmarks. Four reference rankings reuse one Earth catalog rather than repeating elevation extraction four times.

Use a run manifest with dataset identity/version, original URL, checksum, registration interpretation, Earth radius and window parameters, mask identity/version/license, center-grid algorithm/order/IDs, software version, NoData policy, descriptor schema, and calibration membership. A catalog row records center, footprint ID, source validity, land/ice category, descriptor values/units, quality/exclusion reason, and source tile hashes. Maintain per-shard checkpoints with atomic output and integrity checks; rerunning identical inputs should produce identical ordered IDs and values. Prefer a simple versioned table format with explicit schema and checksums; select Parquet only after measuring whether its dependency and columnar access help. Keep raw raster, manifests, catalogs and diagnostics separate. Source inputs are immutable, and revised masks or policies produce a new catalog rather than overwrite one.

## 7. Preimplementation validation and acceptance evidence

1. **Source equivalence:** independently inspect global raster dimensions, node coordinates, orientation, NoData and values against authoritative metadata and all seven cached ERDDAP windows. Reproduce every existing window's sample values and three descriptors (within justified numerical tolerance) before comparing ranks. Any discrepancy gets a documented cause and separate catalog version.
2. **Geometry and masks:** synthetic rasters/polygons check node versus pixel registration, latitude-dependent footprint width, land below sea level, coasts, lakes, islands, zero/partial valid samples, high latitudes, both poles, longitude seam and tile halo. Independently recompute selected real windows and area fractions.
3. **Coverage and bias:** publish global center manifest, land/mixed/ice/excluded counts and map, eligible area coverage by latitude band and continent, nearest-center gap diagnostics, and overlap/duplicate statistics. Centers are globally rule-generated; selection and exclusions are reproducible. “Systematic” requires these checks, not just many candidate coordinates.
4. **Numerical and scientific stability:** run all four existing references, compare seven legacy windows as controls, separate changes due to new source bytes, center population and IQR calibration, test 32 versus 64 grids and shifted centers, descriptor-subset and spatial-block holdout sensitivity, and the existing density/extent experiment. Report full ranks, top-position overlap, rank correlation over **matched** candidates, range diagnostics, feature contributions and exclusion reasons. Distances from different calibrations are not directly comparable.
5. **Operational checks:** verify deterministic rerun hashes, interruption/resume without duplicate IDs, bounded memory, tile-edge continuity, API pagination/read-only behavior and source attribution. Time and storage measurements come from a pilot, never from the estimates above.

Passing these tests would support a systematic **topographic nearest-window search**. It would still be insufficient to claim newly discovered or validated geological, environmental, or operational planetary analogs; those claims require independent field/context evidence and a separately defined validation framework.

## 8. Risks, unresolved questions and staged roadmap

| Risk or decision | Consequence | Proposed resolution before adoption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional ERDDAP versus global ETOPO1 registration/values | Historical baseline may not reproduce | Independent seven-window pixel and descriptor comparison; version differences explicitly. |
| Composite effective resolution and source bias | Similar numbers may represent unlike terrain support | Source lineage audit and controlled scale/source sensitivity; no artificial precision. |
| Mixed sea/land and ice surfaces | Bathymetry or ice top could masquerade as exposed land terrain | Independent polygon classification, explicit separate catalogs and policy review. |
| Grid density versus missed terrain/duplicate windows | Ranking depends on center placement and overlap | Center-gap maps and 32/64/shifted-grid pilot, with geographic grouping only for presentation. |
| Catalog-dependent IQR and outliers | Distances/ranks change even for unchanged windows | Immutable calibration manifest; block/strata sensitivity; no cross-catalog score equivalence. |
| Pole and seam geometry | Wrong footprint or omitted windows | Synthetic and real edge controls, validated geodesic footprint logic. |
| Unknown I/O and storage costs | Large downloads or slow processing | Small bounded tile pilot, measured resources, resumable batch design. |

**Stage 0 — design approval:** choose source/version, coastline mask, eligibility policy, grid pilot and storage budget. **Stage 1 — bounded pilot:** acquire only necessary source portions, independently verify seven legacy controls and difficult geography; measure throughput. **Stage 2 — controlled global discovery:** acquire/version a full source only after pilot, generate deterministic centers and resumable features, calibrate and rank in a separate artifact. **Stage 3 — independent validation:** coverage, scale, calibration and geographic-block sensitivity; publish exclusions and limitations. **Stage 4 — optional targeted follow-up:** investigate higher-detail elevation and independent non-topographic evidence at shortlisted sites in a separately approved phase. No stage is marked complete by this document.

## Pilot Specification and Acceptance Criteria

It does **not** produce a global catalog, global ranking, or a chosen final HEALPix density. All numerical limits in this section are engineering caps or experimental settings, not scientific similarity/quality thresholds.

For each `Nside` (32 and 64), define the original HEALPix **NESTED** scheme and a reproducible shifted variant: rotate the center *unit vectors* by half of `sqrt(4 * pi / (12 * Nside**2))` radians about the Earth-fixed x axis, then by the same angle about the z axis. Save the exact rotation matrix/order and code/library version. This is a phase-sensitivity experiment, not a newly optimized grid. A future pilot samples **at most 16 centers from each of the four manifests** (32, rotated 32, 64, rotated 64): one nearest center to each of the eight additional controls, then a second center from each test region if distinct and inside its bounded raster tile; ties by stable pixel ID. A missing second eligible center is recorded, not silently replaced with a distant one. Test-center selection precedes inspecting ranks. The global theoretical counts (12,288 and 49,152) are formula-derived; **do not generate full global manifests in this pilot**. Report actual 16-or-fewer selected IDs, mask categories, selection distances, overlap and eligibility counts **only in tested regions**. The up-to-seven legacy plus eight additional controls plus 64 grid samples give **no more than 79 unique first-resolution window evaluations** before deduplicating shared centers. Perform seven extra coarse Earth window evaluations; the extraction cap is **86 windows / 260,150 bilinear query positions** at the unchanged 3,025 per window. Independent validation can read those same source pixels again; track its additional I/O separately.

For the regional coverage diagnostic, the 16 extracted windows per manifest alone do **not** suffice to find the nearest eligible center. Query neighboring HEALPix IDs analytically from each fixed evaluation point, classify their footprints from the locally available masks without extracting elevations, and expand the search until the nearest eligible center is proved nearer than any unvisited center. Cap this auxiliary eligibility work at **512 center checks per manifest**; report a distance as *censored/unresolved* if the search reaches the data boundary or cap. This bound applies to the pilot, not a later global completeness audit. Distinguish all-center nearest distance, eligibility-filtered nearest distance and selected-extraction-center distance.

The up-to-16 regional elevation-subset requests must each be clipped to the union of assigned test footprints plus the documented interpolation/aggregation halo, with per-request response budget **20 MiB**, total acquired bytes **256 MiB** (including masks), temporary disk **1 GiB**, and measured peak process memory target **1 GiB**. These are proposed **stop rules and resource budgets**, not observed performance. Use existing cached CSV bytes where possible. Do not fetch an entire high-resolution ice mask or global DEM merely to satisfy a test. For a high-latitude or pole-crossing bounding box that would exceed budget, record unsupported/blocked rather than construct an unbounded `1/cos(latitude)` longitude range. If a source's size/access cannot be confirmed in advance, stop its gate. Do not claim a polar mask test passed by replacing it with synthetic data.

### Pilot measurements and experiments

1. **Registration and source equivalence:** compare source-file documentation and independently decoded regional node values with ERDDAP subsets at every existing control and selected seam/polar controls. Reproduce all seven source windows' 3,025 queries and three descriptors from the exact same bytes where the global file or authoritative regional extraction is available. Calculate absolute and relative differences and justify tolerances from encoding and floating-point operations. No equivalence claim without an independent source extract.
2. **Mask and geometry:** synthetic polygons/raster grids assert below-sea-level land, coasts, lakes/islands, holes, ice/mixed classes, boundary-only intersections, nonfinite/NoData, polar singularities, antimeridian polygons, and nonzero-weight interpolation neighbors. Then compare actual pilot classifications with documented GSHHG/GIMP/Antarctic controls; verify reprojection with independent coordinate round trips. Report land/water/ice/unclassified **area**, full-footprint fractions, 3,025 query classes and source-node support. Only strictly complete dry-land footprints with no mapped ice in the versioned inventory epochs may enter a *pilot-only* exposed-land ranking; seasonal snow and unmapped ice remain limitations; do not change production.
3. **Coarse-source sensitivity:** evaluate unmodified 1' versus declared 4' area-weighted ETOPO1 block representation on the seven Earth controls, keeping all four planetary grids fixed. Validate aggregation on a synthetic plane and discontinuity, independently recalculate all three descriptors, and compare rankings once with frozen original IQR and once with refitted Earth IQR. Log per-feature/rank/extrapolation changes and lost local relief; do not choose a winning scale.
4. **Coverage and shift:** in each of the seven legacy and eight additional geographic test regions, create a fixed, independently sampled evaluation set of land points and report its construction, denominator and mask uncertainty. Compare original and rotated center manifests at 32/64 using nearest-eligible-center geodesic distances, latitude/region strata, candidate density per eligible area, uncovered points, footprint intersection-over-union and near-duplicate frequency across *explicit trial overlap values*. Record how each changed center alters sampled windows/descriptors. No pilot-only estimate is relabeled global coverage.
5. **Performance and resume:** measure total bytes, bytes per subset, raster-cache hit rate, wall-clock time per window and tile, peak memory, temporary storage, checkpoint writes, and dependency versions on the actual host. Interrupt after a completed shard and restart; assert identical source hashes, center IDs, descriptors, ordering and exclusion reasons with no duplicated records. Compare repeated cold/warm executions separately.

### Acceptance gates and decisions after the pilot

| Gate | Evidence required for a bounded-pilot pass | If not met |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raster identity and registration | Authoritative extracted source values, node centers and seven-window queries agree with the legacy ERDDAP controls within a **derived, documented numerical tolerance**; discrepancies have an independently checked explanation. | Keep catalogs separate; do not infer equivalence or alter baseline. |
| Class integrity | Synthetic cases pass; real ice/coast/lake masks align after CRS conversion; every output has area fractions and node/kernel validity, with unresolved regions explicitly excluded from the exposed-land cohort. | Mark affected geography unclassifiable; no exposed-land ranking from it. |
| Coarse experiment | Original bytes retained; block-support algorithm independently checked; all three descriptors and both calibration variants produced on seven controls, including differences and NoData behavior. | Report experiment inconclusive; no aggregation recommendation. |
| HEALPix diagnostic | Four bounded manifest samples have deterministic IDs, center count and class counts; same evaluation points compared; region/latitude gap, overlap and shifted-grid effects reported with denominators. | Do not choose a grid density. Even if passed, global grid choice still needs globe-wide evaluation. |
| Computation | No cap violated; memory/time/I/O measured; interrupted run resumes with identical hashes/results and no duplicate window IDs. | Revise pilot data access/checkpoint architecture before a global run. |
| Scientific reporting | Baseline output unchanged; experimental distances have their own calibration identity; excluded and inconclusive cases visible; no percent similarity or analog-validity claim. | Do not publish global discovery claims. |

A pilot may pass its nonpolar engineering gates while the authenticated regional ice data remain inaccessible; **the ice/class gate then fails for the affected polar footprints**. These are independent gates, not a score. Neither a passed pilot nor a stable nearest candidate proves that global coverage, effective source resolution or planetary analogy is adequate.

### Decisions requiring human approval before implementation

- Approve ETOPO1 grid-registered Ice Surface as the discovery source, select the verified compressed netCDF product or an explicitly validated alternative format, and decide how to obtain an authoritative small extract for the equivalence gate.
- Approve the coastline/ice classification source, land-only policy, treatment of mixed windows and any empirically evaluated valid-data rule.
- Approve an equal-area grid pilot at `Nside=32` and `64`, its geographic gap/stability acceptance criteria, and a bounded data/network/runtime budget.
- Decide whether the existing 220 km computation is retained verbatim for discovery or a separately versioned scale is evaluated after the pilot; approve any future production change explicitly.
- Approve the offline catalog schema, raw-data retention, provenance requirements and how validated global results may later appear in the API/UI. No production ranking or original project migration is authorized here.
